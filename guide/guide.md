Welcome. This book is the manual, and it is also a demonstration. It arrived with three highlights and one comment already in it, so every part of Backchat has something to show you from the first minute.

To turn the page, tap its right edge, press the right arrow key, or swipe left on a phone. To jump between chapters, tap Contents {{icon:contents}} at the top of the screen. Every icon in this book is drawn from the button it describes, so the picture you see here is the picture you will find there.

Read the first three chapters and you can use Backchat. The rest is a map for the day you wonder what a button does. When you have finished with this book, delete it from its book page. It stays gone unless you ask for it back in Settings.

## Why this exists

Most reading apps store books. This one keeps your side of the conversation. This stops being a library and becomes a conversation between you and every mind you have invited in.

Highlights mark the ideas you claimed. Comments hold the person you were when the words first landed. No machine can give back your thoughts about a book, and that part only exists if you write it down.

Everything you mark lives in files you own. No account, no subscription, no cloud deciding whether you can have your words back.

## Start here

Five moves, and you are reading the Backchat way.

1. **Add a book.** On the home screen, tap + Add EPUB, or drop an EPUB file anywhere on the page.
2. **Mark a line.** Open the book, select a sentence, and tap a colour. That is a highlight.
3. **Answer it.** Select a passage and tap the speech bubble {{icon:comment}} to write a comment on it.
4. **Write in the margins.** Tap {{icon:margins-panel}} at the top of the reader. These blank pages belong to the whole book, not to one passage.
5. **Find it again.** Type a few words you remember into the search box on the home screen. Tap a result and the book opens on the exact spot.

One habit protects all of it. Your library lives in this browser until you give it somewhere safer, so tap Save {{icon:save}} on the home screen now and then, or connect a folder in Settings. The chapter Keeping everything safe explains both.

Everything starts here. Along the top, from left to right:

- **The Backchat name** is the sync light. It stays solid when everything is saved, pulses while it saves, and dims with the words Sync paused when it needs you; tap it then to put things right. Hold the pointer over it to see when it last synced. The small mark beside it opens jonathandutoit.com.
- **The search box** looks through everything you have. The chapter Find anything shows how.
- {{icon:save}} **Save** writes your whole library to one file.
- {{icon:load}} **Load** brings any Backchat backup back.
- {{icon:handoff}} **Handoff** carries your newest marks to another device, and its number counts the marks waiting to go. It steps aside when a folder or sync between devices does the carrying for you.
- {{icon:marks}} **All marks** opens every highlight and comment you have made, in every book.
- {{icon:settings}} **Settings** holds the theme, your folder, sync between devices, and the Advanced tools.
- **+ Add EPUB** adds books.

Below the bar sit the daily highlight, a Continue card for the book you are reading, and your five most recent books. Tap a cover to open that book's page. **Library** shows every book you have. **Connections** shows which books your links have tied together.

## Add your books

Backchat reads EPUB, the open format most ebooks come in. Tap + Add EPUB, or drop the files anywhere on the home screen. A book locked to one shop's app (DRM) will not open here; an unlocked EPUB opens in any reader, this one included.

To turn a video into a book, use the [Video Epub Creator for Backchat](https://jonathandutoit.com/video-epub-book-for-backchat/), then add the finished file here. For any other format, convert it to EPUB first with any converter.

**Library** on the home screen shows every book. Type to filter it. Sort by last opened, date added, title, author or progress, and flip the order with {{icon:sort}}. The heart {{icon:heart}} shows only the books you love.

## A book's page

Tap a cover and you arrive at the book's own page, one step before the book itself.

- The heart {{icon:heart}} on the cover makes it a favourite book, which also keeps it searchable inside.
- **+ add tag** files the book under any word you like. Tags are searchable, and in your exported notes they become links.
- **Continue reading** opens the book where you left off.
- {{icon:export}} **Export this book's notes** writes its highlights and comments as one Markdown file.
- {{icon:backup}} **Save a backup of this book** makes one file that brings this book back whole: the book, every mark, your place.
- {{icon:book-link}} **Copy book link** copies a link to this page, ready to paste into another book's margins.
- {{icon:edit}} **Edit metadata** changes the cover, title, author, year, rating, summary, tags and related books.
- {{icon:refresh}} **Refresh EPUB from folder** reloads the book from your folder after you have edited its file there, in Calibre for example, and keeps every mark.
- {{icon:remove}} **Remove from this device** frees the space a book takes and keeps every mark. It appears when no folder is connected, which usually means a phone.
- {{icon:delete}} **Delete book** removes it, after saving a complete backup first.

Below the buttons, the book's margins appear in full, ready to read or edit.

## Inside a book

The top bar of the reader, from left to right:

- {{icon:back}} **Back** returns to where you came from.
- {{icon:home}} **Home** goes to the home screen.
- {{icon:find}} **Find** searches this book alone.
- {{icon:contents}} **Contents** lists the chapters.
- {{icon:listen}} **Read aloud** appears once you switch it on in Display.
- {{icon:highlights}} **Highlights and comments** lists every mark in this book.
- {{icon:margins-panel}} **Margins** opens the book's blank pages.
- {{icon:display}} **Display** sets how the book looks and sounds.

Along the bottom run the chapter and page, a progress bar, how much you have read with an estimate of the time left, and a small dot: the sync light again.

Turn pages by tapping the left or right edge, with the arrows at the sides, by swiping on a phone, or from the keyboard: the right arrow, Space or Page Down to go forward, the left arrow or Page Up to go back. C opens the contents, H the highlights, S the margins, and Esc closes whatever is open. Press ? to see them all.

On a phone, Back works as it does in any app: it closes whatever is open, then the book.

Follow a link inside a book, a footnote for instance, and a Return button waits at the bottom of the page. Tap it to go back to where you were reading.

## Read your way

Display {{icon:display}} holds the settings for reading.

- **Layout.** Page turns like paper. Scroll flows down the screen, one chapter at a time: at the end of a chapter, tap Next to carry on, and to go back a chapter, pull down at the top of one.
- **Theme.** Light, Sepia, Dark, or Auto, which follows your device.
- **Font, font size, line spacing and margin.** Yours to set, and they apply to every book.
- **Original styling.** This one is set per book. Original shows the book exactly as its maker designed it, with its own fonts, colours and layout, which suits books built on diagrams or coloured text. Reader puts your own settings back.
- **Read aloud.** See the chapter Listen.

Change any of them in the middle of a chapter and the words you were reading stay on the screen.

## Mark what matters

Select any text and a small menu appears. It starts with four colours, and each colour carries a meaning, so a glance at a page tells you why you marked it. From left to right:

- {{icon:swatch-yellow}} **Yellow:** I want to remember this.
- {{icon:swatch-green}} **Green:** I want to create from this.
- {{icon:swatch-blue}} **Blue:** I want to use this later.
- {{icon:swatch-pink}} **Pink:** I want to link this to something else.

The order never changes, so the meaning never depends on telling the colours apart. Hold the pointer over a colour to see its meaning.

After the colours come the tools.

The speech bubble {{icon:comment}} attaches a comment to the selected passage. A comment on its own underlines the words without colouring them. Hold the pointer over them, or tap them on a phone, to read it.

The link icon {{icon:location}} copies the passage's location. Paste a location inside any comment and you have linked two places together; tapping the link later jumps straight to that passage, even in another book.

The quote marks {{icon:quote}} copy the selection as a quote, ready to paste into the margins.

The notebook {{icon:margins}} sends the selection straight into the book's margins: the quote, and a location link back to it. Nothing is highlighted. Use it for a line you want to return to but would not colour.

The magnifier {{icon:search}} searches every book for the selected words. The results open over the page, so you keep your place.

Tap a highlight you have already made and the menu returns with fewer, different tools: the colours to recolour it, the star {{icon:star}} to make it a favourite, the speech bubble {{icon:comment}} to add or edit its comment, and the bin {{icon:trash}} to remove it.

## The highlights panel

Highlights and comments {{icon:highlights}} lists every mark in the book, in the order you made them. Along the top, choose All, one colour, only marks with a comment {{icon:comment}}, or only starred marks {{icon:star}}, and search the words in your marks. Export .md writes them all as one Markdown file.

Under each mark: Comment (Edit comment once it has one), Copy, Location, Margins, Rhymes and Delete, with the star beside the text. Copy puts the mark on your clipboard as a Markdown quote that names its book. Margins is the shortcut worth learning: one tap sends the quote and its live location into the book's margins, along with any comment you wrote on it, and a blank line waiting for your note.

Tap the words of any mark and the book turns to it. This guide already has three marks in this panel, one carrying a comment. Go and look, then delete them when you are done.

## The margins

Margins {{icon:margins-panel}} opens blank pages that belong to you. Highlights and comments belong to a passage; the margins belong to the whole book.

There is a method worth stealing here, and it comes from Tim Ferriss. He reads with a pencil, scribbling in the margins as he goes. When he finishes, he builds his own index on the blank front pages: a page number and a few words for every idea worth returning to. Weeks later he comes back and stars the ideas he actually used. Three hundred pages become a handful of actions. [Watch him explain it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YQOrqAKKcUQ).

The margins are those blank front pages, and the method carries over with one upgrade. Paper needs page numbers; you have locations. Mark and comment while you read. When you finish, open the margins and write your index: a line per idea, with the passage's location link pasted beside it. Or let the Margins button under any highlight write the entry for you: quote, link, and a blank line for your words. Where his index says page 212, yours is a live link that jumps straight to the passage. Then let the second pass happen in the marks themselves: star the highlights you find yourself using, and the stars become your distillation.

Tap Edit to write and Done to read. Quotes you copied paste cleanly, and markdown works: headings, bold, lists, block quotes, tables, highlighted and struck-through text. A table is rows of cells between pipes, with a line of dashes under the first row, as Obsidian writes it. Links become tappable, an image link shows the image, and a YouTube link becomes a small card that plays right there in the margins. Paste the plain video link; that is all it takes. The book's page shows its margins in full, with room to write.

## Links between passages

Any passage, highlight or book can be linked to any other. Four buttons copy a link:

- The link icon {{icon:location}} in the selection menu copies a passage.
- **Location** in the highlights panel copies a highlight.
- The link icon {{icon:location}} beside any search result copies that result.
- **Copy book link** {{icon:book-link}} on a book's page copies the book.

Paste the link into a comment or the margins and it becomes tappable. A passage link opens the book on that passage. A highlight link shows the highlight first, with Open in book to go there. A book link opens the book's page. Follow one from a book's page, and Back {{icon:back}} brings you back to those margins.

Links hold. Each one carries the book's identity rather than its place on your shelf, and a passage link carries a few of the passage's words, so a link finds its way back even after the book is added again.

Two places show the web you are weaving. **Connections**, on the home screen, lists your books by the links between them. **Related books**, in Edit metadata, ties books together by hand, and they appear under Related on each book's page. When you export your notes to Markdown, every link becomes a [[wikilink]] that Obsidian understands.

## Find anything

The search box on the home screen looks through your titles, authors, tags, highlights, comments, and the text inside your books. Results arrive in groups: Books, Highlights, Notes and Inside books.

Type several words and you find passages holding all of them, in any order. Put words in quotes, "like this", to find that exact phrase. Press / on the home screen to jump straight to the box.

Inside books covers every book you have opened on this device, and the heart {{icon:heart}} on a book keeps it searchable.

Tap any result and the book opens on the exact spot, with the words briefly tinted so your eye finds them. Inside a book, Find {{icon:find}} searches that book alone, and each match lands on its own place in the chapter.

## All your marks in one place

All marks {{icon:marks}} on the home screen opens every highlight and comment you have ever made, across every book, newest first. Filter by words, by book, by colour, by comments or by stars. Tap any mark to jump to its passage.

Each mark has a Rhymes button. Rhymes are echoes: passages from your other books that share the same idea. Rhymes only appear across books, so mark a second book and watch the connections begin.

## The daily highlight

Each day the home screen resurfaces one thing you marked, along with its rhymes from other books. Shuffle draws another. Reading it takes ten seconds. It is the difference between a pile of finished books and a library that keeps talking.

## Favourites

Two marks of affection, two scopes. The star {{icon:star}} on a highlight favours a passage, and every list of marks can show the starred ones alone. The heart {{icon:heart}} on a book's cover favours the book: the heart in the Library shows only those books, and a favourite book stays searchable inside. Favourites travel with your marks through every sync and handoff.

## Listen

Read aloud lives in Display {{icon:display}}. Turn it on and choose a voice: the player settles at the foot of the page and stays there, and the speaker {{icon:listen}} joins the top bar. Tap play to start with the first sentence on the page, or tap any paragraph to start there.

The sentence being spoken is highlighted, and the page turns with the voice, word by word, so you can read along. The tracking learns each voice's pace, so it keeps step even with voices that report nothing as they speak. It carries on into the next chapter by itself.

To mark a passage, pause, then select, highlight and comment as usual. Press play and the voice picks up at the start of the sentence it was in. Press on the page to select words while it is still reading and the page holds still until you are done, then catches up. Turning the page yourself pauses the voice: play takes you back to it, and a tap on a paragraph starts there instead. The screen stays on while you listen, and through a pause for as long as you keep using the page.

The bar at the bottom steps back or forward a paragraph, plays, pauses and stops, and sets the speed. Save my spot pins the exact sentence; Back to my spot returns there, and if you were listening when you tapped it, the voice resumes from that very sentence.

## Video books

A book made from a video with the [Video Epub Creator for Backchat](https://jonathandutoit.com/video-epub-book-for-backchat/) carries the video with it. A player sits above the text: play, pause, and drag along the bar, while the line being spoken is marked as it plays. Tap any time in the text to play from there. Follow keeps the page turning with the talk, and the player folds away when you only want to read. Mark and comment as in any book.

If the video is taken down, the player says so and folds away. The book, its times and your marks all stay.

## Your folder is the library

On a computer, in Chrome, Edge or Brave, open Settings {{icon:settings}} and tap Connect a folder {{icon:folder}}. Choose a folder inside your Obsidian vault if you use one. From that moment the folder is the source of truth.

Your books are written into a books folder inside it, and the door swings both ways: drop any EPUB into that books folder and it joins your library on the next sync. Your highlights and comments are written as plain Markdown into a highlights folder, one file per book, with your tags and titles as links, readable in Obsidian or any text editor. These Markdown files are an export, not the source: the app rewrites them on every sync, so any edits made there are overwritten. Edit in the app, where your marks and location links stay live.

The browser keeps only a working copy of your twenty most recently opened books. Everything else lives in the folder and is fetched the moment you open it. Clearing the browser loses nothing, because the folder holds the library.

The Backchat name on the home screen is the sync light, and the dot at the bottom of the reader repeats it. In Settings, under Advanced, Check folder reads the folder back to you: which books and notes are present, what is waiting, what is left over, with one tap to clean up files that nothing reads any more. And once a week, with the folder connected, a small backup writes itself into a backups folder, keeping the four newest.

## Sync your devices

Handoffs work, but they rely on you remembering. Backchat can keep your phone and your computer in step on its own, through a small relay you own: a free Cloudflare Worker you set up once. The steps are in the [sync setup guide](https://github.com/wisdomreboot/backchat/blob/main/docs/SYNC-SETUP.md), and they take about fifteen minutes in a browser. Make a token, in Settings, creates the access token for you to paste into Cloudflare.

In Settings, under Sync between devices, give Backchat the relay's address, its access token, and a passphrase you choose. From then on every highlight, comment, margin note, tag, deletion and reading position travels by itself, encrypted on the device before it leaves, so the relay only ever holds scrambled data. Books do not travel: add each EPUB once on each device, and its marks are already waiting. With sync on, the Handoff button steps aside, because there is nothing left to carry.

To add a second device, tap Copy setup link for another device, send the link to yourself, and open it on the second device. The address and token fill in, and you type only the passphrase. Treat that link like a password.

Your library is the collection; a device can hold just the books you are reading. To free space, open a book's page and tap Remove from this device {{icon:remove}}. Only the file leaves; the cover stays, dimmed, with every mark still in place, and adding the EPUB again picks up exactly where you were. Delete is different: with sync on it is labelled Delete everywhere, because it removes the book from every device.

## Set up your phone

With sync between devices, a phone needs two steps. Open the setup link on it and type your passphrase. Then tap + Add EPUB and add the books you want with you. Each one arrives with its marks already in it.

On an iPhone, add Backchat to your home screen first: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Safari can clear the stored data of a website you have not opened for a while; an app on the home screen keeps it.

Without sync, the phone can still share your folder's library. Its browser can read and mark, but it is not allowed to write into your folder, so setting it up takes three steps, once.

First, sync the folder itself. Whatever already syncs your vault carries the library: iCloud, Syncthing, Dropbox, or Obsidian Sync. One warning if you use Obsidian Sync: in its settings, turn on syncing for all file types, or the EPUB files will not travel to the phone.

Second, get your marks in first. On the phone, tap Load {{icon:load}} and pick the newest file from the folder's backups folder; the weekly automatic backup means a fresh one is always waiting. Your whole library arrives as marks, comments, positions and settings. The covers appear dimmed with a small download arrow: the marks are home, the pages are not on the phone yet.

Third, get the books in. Tap + Add EPUB and pick the books you actually want on the phone from the synced books folder in your files app. Each one matches its marks and comes into colour. Loading before adding keeps the phone's copy of each book identical to the computer's, so everything later merges by the strongest possible match. Repeat Load whenever you want the phone brought up to date.

## Bring the train home

Without sync, read and mark freely on the phone. Everything is held safely on the device.

When you are done, tap Handoff {{icon:handoff}} on the home screen. That makes one small file holding the books you touched since the last handoff. The button keeps a count, so the phone tells you when there is something worth sending. Hand off at the end of each session; until the computer collects it, that file is also the phone's backup. Save it into the top level of your synced folder, the same level as the books and highlights folders, not inside them. The computer looks only there.

On the next sync at your computer, the file is merged into your library and removed. Marks made at home and marks made on the train never fight, because each book merges item by item and the newest edit of each mark wins.

## Keeping everything safe

Three buttons sit together on the home screen. {{icon:save}} Save writes your whole library to one file. {{icon:load}} Load brings any backup back. {{icon:handoff}} Handoff passes your latest marks to another device, and only shows when nothing syncs for you.

There are really only two things you ever do with your data, and each word means one thing.

Save and Restore are a round trip. A Save always makes a file you can bring back, whether it holds your whole library, a single book, or a book you just deleted. And Restore reads any of them. Load {{icon:load}} on the home screen and Restore from a file, in Settings under Advanced, are the same action: hand either one any Backchat backup and it works out what is inside and merges it. Nothing already here is removed, and a book you deleted stays deleted unless you restore it on purpose.

Export {{icon:export}} is the other thing, and it never comes back. Export to Markdown, in Settings under Advanced, makes a readable copy of your reading for a notes app like Obsidian. It is for reading elsewhere, not for restoring, and it says so.

With a folder {{icon:folder}} connected, Save keeps the file small by storing only your marks, comments, progress and settings, because the folder already holds the books. Without a folder, the books ride along too.

## Deleting is safe

Delete a book from its page and Backchat saves a complete backup of it first: the file, your highlights, comments and reading position, into the backups folder or your downloads. Bring it back later with Load {{icon:load}} and the book returns whole, open to the same page. So you can clear a finished book off your shelf without ever losing the words you wrote.

One button goes further, and it says so. Delete everything in this browser, at the bottom of Settings under Advanced, erases this browser's whole library. It leaves your folder untouched, and it offers to download a backup before it starts.

## Where Backchat lives

Backchat is one free web page at [backchat.jonathandutoit.com](https://backchat.jonathandutoit.com). Its code is public on GitHub at [github.com/wisdomreboot/backchat](https://github.com/wisdomreboot/backchat), and every line that runs is published there; the page refuses to run anything that is not. There you can read what changed in each build, how your data is handled, how to set up sync, and how to host your own copy. To report a problem or suggest something, open an issue there. The build you are running is named at the bottom of Settings, under Advanced.

No account, no cookies, no record of what you read. The page does count how often it is opened, through GoatCounter: no cookies, and nothing from your books, notes or folder. To leave yourself out of that count, open the page once with #toggle-goatcounter at the end of its address.
